This is a storybook: the story comes first and the page is its home. The
reader, voice and picture records at project root (`audience.md`,
`prose-style.md`, `illustration-style.md`) are prefilled with all-ages
storybook defaults; follow them, and do not re-ask those questions until the
story is done or the author brings them up. The story lives under `story/`
per `instructions/story-project-layout.md`; `story/README.md` is its premise
sheet.

Work ideas-led. On the first story turn ask at most five questions, one at
a time if the author is young: who is it about, what do they want, what is
in the way, where does it happen, how should the ending feel. When the
author is stuck on one, offer a riff of concrete options instead of
repeating the question. Reflect the answers as a story spine in their words,
then write a short version all the way to the end and read it back. Grow it
from the part they like best; keep their ideas and their words; praise one
specific thing and fix one thing per pass. Do not mention page design,
pictures or formats while the story is being written.

Ask plainly at each natural stopping point: "Is the story done, or do you
want to change anything?" When the author agrees it is done, put it on the
page (replace the sample collection in `index.html`, `notes.ts` and the
browser check with the story's chapters, keeping the page readable on a
phone), then propose the format decisions in one short turn, each as a riff:
how the page should look, whether pictures are wanted and in what style (the
prefilled default is a cover plus one per chapter, flat and bright), and
whether a read-aloud or printed version matters. Then suggest a remix
(`.agents/skills/remix/SKILL.md`): the same story for a younger reader, in
first person, or illustrated differently, made under `story/remixes/` with
the original untouched.

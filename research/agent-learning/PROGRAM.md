# Program Agent learning from experience

Investigate when agents become more useful by retrieving, interpreting, and
consolidating experience across tasks. The target is better verified outcomes
for the combined cost of inference, elapsed time, maintenance, and human
attention. Useful discoveries include material nobody explicitly routed to the
agent, provided irrelevant or misleading context does not erase the benefit.

The program has three practical aims:

1. Independently experiment to validate product and community claims, including
   reproductions of advertised results and tests devised around our own work.
2. Improve `~/agents` instructions, meta-instructions, and how graehl approaches
   them. Candidates include structured periodic reviews, a single persistent
   working observer session, and manual review/editing of automatically
   accumulated material. Reduced hands-on effort is an outcome to measure.
3. Implement useful techniques through YA, boot, and harness/MCP facilities
   when their existing implementations require paid-token access. Claude and
   Codex subscription harnesses are the usual constraint; pi for OpenAI models
   is a candidate where supported. Separate a technique's inherent requirements
   from a product's current integration limitations and measure fidelity lost
   in a subscription-compatible implementation.

Two use cases remain separate: very challenging high-value tasks for which
approximately 100 times the usual effort can be justified, and routine work
approaching the effectiveness per cost/time of graehl's manual agent reviews
of `~/agents` with little hands-on involvement. The first can justify rare
breakthroughs; the second must repay recurring overhead.

The scope includes session retrieval, persistent memory, skill acquisition,
instruction revision, review triggers, context selection/injection, and human
curation. Product/community topics distinguish their contribution to these
questions from orthogonal capabilities and judge the applicability of both.
Popularity, elaborate plumbing, emergent behavior, and the number of saved
memories or skills are discovery signals rather than success criteria.

The [working agenda](agenda.md) owns candidate comparisons and monthly evidence
monitoring. The [topic index](GLOSSARY.md) owns product/community entry points;
[current focus](ROOT.md) records execution state. This charter establishes a
research program, without authorizing deployment or expensive experiments.

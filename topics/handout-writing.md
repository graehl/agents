# Handout writing

> Write a decision-focused research handout for an effectively uninterested
> first-time reader by replacing chronological work history with a
> self-contained current claim, evidence, and decision.

Topic: `handout-writing`

## Rewrite for first contact

Research handouts naturally accrete as chronological logs because results
arrive in time order. Before dissemination, run a substantial whole-document
consolidate, review, and revise pass. The mechanism may be a direct revision or
the available dream/review workflow; the required outcome is a new-reader
narrative, not a ritual invocation.

Assume this is the third update but the reader's first moment of attention.
Lead with why the program is worth attention now, the current decision or
claim, the evidence that changed the situation, and the action or judgment the
handout supports. Reorganize or cut the chronology. Merely prepending an
executive summary to an unchanged running log does not complete the pass.

## Restore the context the working session stopped noticing

- Link the program or project glossary near the opening when one exists, while
  still defining dominant jargon and abbreviations at first use.
- Replace run ids, checkpoint nicknames, and internal stage labels with literal
  reader-facing names; preserve the internal handle only as a provenance link.
- Make each table self-decoding: caption the population and comparison, state
  metric direction, split and N, define nonstandard columns, and identify the
  baseline and cost boundary. Put the takeaway in adjacent prose or the
  caption rather than expecting a reader to infer it from bold cells.
- Separate measured results, pending work, deployment assumptions, and
  downstream consequences. An uninterested reader will not repair those links
  charitably.

One useful order is status/source map, decision summary, one-page result,
claim-bearing tables and limitations, current plan or ask, then detailed
research narrative and appendices. Treat it as a test, not a mandatory
template: headings, tables, and captions alone should still communicate the
current decision.

## Relationship to progress reports and papers

Handouts inherit the cold-reader and scanability concerns in
[`progress-report`](progress-report.md), but they are mutable decision
snapshots rather than frozen dated installments. They may be rewritten as the
audience or decision changes. Progress reports retain their stream order and
carry corrections forward; papers additionally make a durable publication
claim under [`paper-drafting`](paper-drafting.md).

The working PII exemplar and its corrective advisor record are
`~/draft/research/pii-redaction-frontier/incumbent-comparison-handout.md` and
`~/draft/research/pii-redaction-frontier/advisor/incumbent-comparison-handout-review.md`.
They are evidence for this guidance, not a universal handout template.

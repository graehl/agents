# frontier-capability-review — evidence

Append-only review history for the selective frontier-capability register.
Ordinary instruction agents need the main topic, not this ledger.

## 2026-08-12 — register established from advisor overcompliance

- **User observation** — research workers were overcompliant with advisor
  comments and spent real time seeking confirmation that the advisor accepted
  a rebuttal. The advisor sees monitored documents and immediately adjacent
  links, while the user and worker normally know more of the live program.
- **Disposition** — retire advisor agreement/convergence as a gate. Preserve
  the advisor as a bounded critical-reviewer proxy: repair truthful document
  gaps, verify concrete methodological or factual objections directly, and
  preserve user-laid plans absent independent contrary evidence.
- **Review mechanism** — major frontier generations and fresh user experience
  are event-driven triggers; a 60-day schedule is a no-op-capable backstop.
  Ordinary traces come first, a bounded presumption switch is the intermediate
  probe, and controlled guidance-A/guidance-B evaluation is reserved for
  explicitly high-value questions.
- **Scope decision** — use a durable topic rather than a never-cleared gap.
  The register may retain retired/downgraded practices and higher-capability-
  only guidance so later reviews can see both directions of change.
- **Trace: scheduled review with no evidence** — sixty days pass without a
  relevant model release or new incident. Literal use records `no change` and
  reschedules; it does not rewrite instructions or launch an ablation to make
  the review look productive.
- **Trace: plausible capability gain** — a new frontier generation appears
  less prone to ungrounded cross-file claims. One suitable session relaxes the
  Opus path-trace tightening as a declared presumption switch, then inspects
  consequential assertions. A clean anecdote can justify another bounded probe
  or a narrow relaxation; it is not promoted into universal proof.
- **Trace: expensive evaluation** — a route-specific delegation rule is
  annoying but has low observed cost. The calendar review retains it without a
  SWE-bench campaign. A high-frequency rule credibly affecting task success may
  be marked high-value and advanced to the controlled ablation design after a
  pilot estimates the noise floor.
